Overview of Data Centers and Energy Consumption

Demand of Data centers (USA)

The United States continues to lead in data center development, fueled by the demand for cloud computing, AI, and digital services. Key insights include:

  • Capacity Expansion: In 2024, the U.S. added 5,000 MW of new data center capacity, equivalent to 1% of the nation's total power consumption.
  • Construction Pipeline: Nearly 6.5 GW is under construction, with 72% pre-leased, indicating strong demand.
  • Regional Growth: Texas is emerging as a significant hub, with 10 data centers under construction in Abilene as part of the $500 billion Stargate project, a collaboration between OpenAI, SoftBank, and Oracle.

Demand of Data centers (INDIA)

India's data center industry is experiencing rapid growth, driven by increasing digitalization, data localization mandates, and the proliferation of internet services. Key highlights include:

  • Capacity Expansion: India's data center capacity reached approximately 1,255 MW between January and September 2024 and is projected to expand further to around 1,600 MW by the end of 2024.
  • Future Projections: The capacity is expected to more than double to 2–2.3 GW by fiscal 2027.
  • Geographical Distribution: Major data center hubs include Navi Mumbai, Noida, Gurgaon, Bangalore, and Hyderabad, with Navi Mumbai alone accounting for 44% of the market.

Challenges

  • The increasing power demand from data centers, especially those supporting AI, is straining existing power infrastructure. Data centers are anticipated to represent 6% of total U.S. electricity consumption by 2026.

  • The rapid growth of data centers is intensifying the strain on India’s already overtaxed power grid. Data centers rely mainly on fossil fuels, contributing to a substantial carbon footprint.

Solutions

  • Many tech and cloud providers are investing in carbon-free energy sources and pushing for net-zero targets. Enhancing data center efficiency, improving coordination between developers and electricity providers, and developing stronger modeling tools for long-term grid investments are also key strategies.

  • Several Indian data centers are adopting renewable energy sources. For instance, ST Telemedia Global Data Centres (STT GDC) sourced 62.5% of its electricity from renewable energy in 2023, far ahead of its target to achieve 60% by 2026. Additionally, AI is being used to optimize energy and mineral supply, electricity generation and transmission, and energy consumption.
